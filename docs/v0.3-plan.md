# v0.4: a second brain you can talk to

*(Was v0.3. It moved back one release: the office/dev merge in
`office-dev-plan.md` comes first.)*

From a coworker's wish list (2026-09-24): "a little coworker sitting on my
desktop that I can casually talk to throughout the day, and its main job is to
help me remember all the little things my brain is juggling."

v0.2.0 (the taskbar release) ships first; this builds on it.

## What it adds

1. **Tell him things out loud.** A global hotkey (default Ctrl+Alt+Space) or a
   mic button beside the + starts listening. He perks up, listens until you
   pause, and answers. Nothing listens in the background.
2. **Local reminders he understands himself.** "Remind me at 3 to call DISA",
   "bark at me about the Stephen drone map before 5", "remind me tomorrow to
   check on Brandon's hotel", "in 20 minutes". Saved instantly, no Claude
   round trip. At the time he pops up, hops and says it. Then he nudges every
   10 minutes, up to 3 times, and after that it waits in *Waiting on you*
   until ticked off.
3. **Claude makes them smart.** Reminders are written to
   `Clawd\reminders.json`. On its next sweep, Claude reads them and links each
   one to the real thing (the hotel booking email, Stephen's Slack thread, the
   DISA contact) as the item's link, with a proposal where one fits. The words
   you said stay exactly as you said them.
4. **A spoken morning briefing.** The digest sweep also writes a short spoken
   rundown: meetings, deadlines, hotel check-ins, first days on site, your
   reminders for today, and outstanding follow-ups. He holds it until you're
   actually at the computer (first keyboard or mouse activity after it
   arrives), then says it once: "Good morning! You have three meetings today.
   Brandon checks into his hotel tonight, Alex's first day at Robinson is
   today, and you wanted to call DISA before 5." Hotel check-ins and first days
   are plain-English custom rules in `prefs.json`, so each person's briefing
   fits their job.
5. **Clipboard memory.** Off until switched on in the gear. When on, he keeps
   today's copied *text* (up to 100 items). It is wiped at midnight, never
   leaves the computer, and is never sent to Claude. He skips anything a
   password manager marks private (the `ExcludeClipboardContentFromMonitorProcessing`
   format). Entries are recognised as phone numbers, emails, addresses,
   plates and plain text, so "what was that number I copied earlier?" shows
   and says the most recent phone-like one, with the rest one click away in
   the popover.

## How the voice works

- **Primary:** Windows' own speech recognition (WinRT
  `Windows.Media.SpeechRecognition`, dictation) called from Rust. Accurate,
  but it usually needs Windows' *Online speech recognition* privacy setting,
  which some work laptops switch off by policy.
- **Fallback:** the classic offline recogniser (SAPI), which has lower
  accuracy but no online dependency.
- **Last resort:** the mic button focuses the Ask box and opens Windows voice
  typing (Win+H).

Whichever is available is used, and the popover says which. What you said
goes to a small local intent parser:

| Intent | Examples | Handled by |
|---|---|---|
| reminder | "remind me…", "bark at me…", "don't let me forget…" | him, locally |
| clipboard | "what was that number/email/address I copied" | him, locally |
| briefing | "what's my day look like", "morning rundown" | him (the last briefing) |
| anything else | "draft a reply to Priya" | handed to Claude, like the Ask box |

The time parser covers what people actually say: "at 3" (the next 3 o'clock
in work hours), "before 5" (15 minutes before, so there's time to act), "by
end of day" (the end of work hours from `prefs.json`), "tomorrow" (tomorrow at
the start of work hours), "tomorrow at 10", "in 20 minutes / an hour",
"Monday", and "this afternoon". With no time given he asks "When should I
remind you?" and listens once more.

## Order of work

1. **Voice spike on both laptops.** Can WinRT dictation run here, and on the
   work machine? This decides primary versus fallback. (Added to
   `phase0-checklist.md`.)
2. **Reminders (typed first):** the time parser with tests, `reminders.json`,
   the due/nudge flow on the existing inbox (a local item of kind `reminder`
   with `source: "you"`), and "remind me…" in the Ask box.
3. **Voice:** the hotkey, the mic button, recognition, and routing into the
   same intents. A listening pose (the peek clip's alert frame until one is
   drawn).
4. **Clipboard memory:** the Rust watcher (clipboard sequence number,
   exclusion formats, text only), classification, the popover section, and
   the "what did I copy" intent.
5. **Briefing:** the skill writes a `briefing` item. The pet holds it until
   there's activity, then speaks it once per day.
6. **The skill:** read `reminders.json`, enrich reminders, and new custom-rule
   examples (hotel check-ins, first days on site).

## Privacy, stated plainly

- The microphone is on only after the hotkey or a click, and only until you
  stop talking.
- Clipboard memory is opt-in, text only, today only, local only, and skips
  private content.
- Reminders live in the Clawd folder, which Claude reads during a sweep so it
  can add context. That is the point of them, and the setup explains it.
